### Briefing (~200 words)

Today’s through-line is straightforward: the EEA Joint Committee continues to “keep pace” with the EU’s financial-services and corporate-law acquis by updating what is legally incorporated into the EEA Agreement. Three separate decisions—each amending Annex IX (financial services) and, in one case, Annex XXII (company law)—signal that regulatory alignment is not a one-off exercise but an ongoing process that directly reshapes the compliance landscape in Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway [1]–[3].

What changed is the content of the annexes themselves: the EU measures listed in Annex IX (and Annex XXII) are updated or inserted, meaning the corresponding obligations become enforceable within the EEA/EFTA legal order. For firms, the implication is operational rather than theoretical. Authorisation conditions, reporting duties, conduct-of-business requirements and supervisory expectations can all shift when the incorporated EU text changes. That typically forces adjustments to internal controls, documentation and supervisory reporting lines, even where the underlying EU framework was already familiar to market participants.

For regulators and supervisors, the update matters because it provides a harmonised domestic legal basis for oversight across the EEA. In practice, this reduces fragmentation and supports consistent supervisory outcomes, but it also increases the need for timely implementation and guidance to ensure firms can comply without regulatory uncertainty.
