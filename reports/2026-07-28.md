**[1] [Central banks, debt managers, and specialness in the Bund repo market](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3259~ca31e19d1b.en.pdf)**

- Elevated repo rate specialness for German government bonds in 2016–17, and particularly in 2022-23, has often been linked to the absorption of these securities by the ECB’s asset purchase programmes.
- We provide the first evidence on how the debt management office mitigates these effects by jointly analyzing daily secondary-market trades and repo operations of the Deutsche Finanzagentur (DFA) alongside Eurosystem transactions in Bunds from 2015–2024.
- We find two points: first, that Eurosystem purchases depress repo rates about four times more than DFA purchases (0.4 bp vs 0.1 bp per 1% of free float), while DFA repo lending raises repo rates by roughly 0.2 bp per 1% of outstanding volume.
- Our evidence suggests that DFA interventions helped mitigate scarcity-induced specialness.

**[35] [The effects of a large energy price shock on bank credit](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3260~7f05446cac.en.pdf)**

- This study investigates the effect of the large shock to energy prices following the Russian invasion of Ukraine on bank credit to firms.
- To isolate the causal effect of the shock, it compares bank lending to high-energy-intensive firms to that of similar low-energy-intensive firms.
- Following the shock, bank credit to high-energy-intensive firms persistently declined, while their interest rates on new loans rose and other loan terms tightened.
- Across the distribution, safer firms reduced outstanding credit lines and paid unchanged interest rates on new bank loans, while riskier firms borrowed at higher interest rates.
