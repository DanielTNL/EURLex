**[1] [Transatlantic securitisation gap revisited: comparing EU and US securitisation architectures](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op403.en.pdf)**

- This paper revisits the transatlantic gap in securitisation issuance.
- In comparing the securitisation frameworks in the European Union (EU) and United States, we adopt a granular approach, distinguishing between instrument types and considering the broader funding and risk transfer frameworks in which securitisation operates.
- We conclude that the apparent gap in terms of scale stems from methodological and structural differences in how securitisation is defined and measured across jurisdictions, rather than from a structurally weaker or less developed EU securitisation market.
- In particular, commonly cited comparisons of securitisation volumes often exclude covered bond issuance, despite covered bonds being a significant and longstanding component of asset-backed funding in the EU.

- ​The European Banking Authority (EBA) today published two Reports on the market share of subsidiaries of non-EU banks in the EU, as well as on EU banks’ assets and liabilities in foreign currencies.
- As of December 2025, subsidiaries of third country banking groups accounted for 10.22% of total EU banking assets, up from 9.8% a year earlier.
- The Reports also show that EU banks increased foreign currency funding  to 25.3% of total funding, up from 24.8% as of December 2024, helping to reduce currency mismatches and largely eliminate the mismatch in US dollar positions.
