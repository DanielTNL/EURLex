**[2] [A theory of bank liquidity requirements](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3252~91761af52b.en.pdf)**

- We develop a general equilibrium theory of financial intermediation and its implications for liquidity regulation.
- The model is built around an agency problem arising from leveraged intermediation: banks finance loan origination with deposits and face moral hazard in risk management, while holding cash mitigates these incentives at the cost of foregone investment returns.
- Liquidity demand therefore emerges endogenously from incentive considerations rather than from exposure to exogenous funding shocks.
- In equilibrium, financial experts choose between allocating equity to the banking sector relative to the non-bank financial sector that can provide ex-post liquidity by buying bank assets.
