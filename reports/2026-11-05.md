**[4] [ECB perception of non-banks: a machine learning-based approach](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3304~9082a5f181.en.pdf)**

- This paper studies the European Central Bank’s (ECB) perception of non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) and how this perception is publicly communicated.
- We construct a perception index based on ECB speeches over the period 1998–2025.
- Sentiment in references to non-banks is classified by a large language model that we fine-tune on our own hand-labeled dataset constructed for this purpose.
- The resulting index reveals a predominantly negative and declining perception over time, particularly since the Global Financial Crisis.

**[10] [Deep-π*: a neural network estimate of trend inflation](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3307~d3d2e0c5e4.en.pdf)**

- Most empirical models of trend inflation treat it as a latent random walk driven by unobserved shocks, so they cannot say which economic conditions move it.
- Deep- π*makes the innovations to the trend a possibly nonlinear function of a large number of macroeconomic indicators, estimated with neural networks, so that Shapley values can be used to attribute every movement of the trend to the indicators behind it.
- Two complementary loss functions define a current trend, which fits inflation today, and a forward-looking trend, which best predicts inflation over the next year.
- In US data, employment-related variables were the largest contributors to the post-pandemic rise of the current trend.
