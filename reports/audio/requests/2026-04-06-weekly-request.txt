The key point is not a single new rule, but the *mechanism* ESMA uses to promote **supervisory convergence**—i.e., making sure national competent authorities (NCAs) interpret and apply EU financial rules in broadly the same way. The page reiterates ESMA’s powers under **Article 16 of the ESMA Regulation (1095/2010)**: ESMA can issue **guidelines and recommendations** addressed to competent authorities and, where relevant, to market participants. It also highlights that ESMA can run **open public consultations** where appropriate during development, and that it can request information on whether authorities comply—then **publish reasons for non-compliance**. That “name-and-explain” element is a quiet but powerful driver of consistency. On the technical standards side, the page explains ESMA’s ability under **Articles 10 et seq. of Regulation 1095/2010** to elaborate technical standards submitted to the Commission for endorsement.
