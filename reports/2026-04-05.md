### Briefing (~200 words)

Today's European policy briefing underscores a pivotal moment in the regulatory landscape, particularly within the banking and insurance sectors, while also addressing the broader implications of climate change on financial stability.

The appointment of François-Louis Michaud as Chair of the European Banking Authority (EBA), effective April 16, 2026, signals a forthcoming shift in EU banking regulation. Michaud's leadership is anticipated to recalibrate the EBA's focus on financial stability and consumer protection, potentially altering the regulatory priorities that govern banks across the EU. This transition period allows for strategic preparation, especially as the banking sector faces evolving economic challenges and regulatory demands [1].

In parallel, the final report on Solvency II introduces updated supervisory reporting and public disclosure requirements aimed at enhancing transparency in the insurance sector. By streamlining reporting processes and improving data quality, the revisions seek to alleviate administrative burdens while bolstering stakeholder confidence. This initiative is crucial for fostering better risk management practices within insurance companies, which are increasingly vital in a climate-affected economic environment [2].

Moreover, recent studies highlight the nuanced relationship between severe weather events and financial stability, revealing that while these shocks do not drive business cycles, they can deteriorate capital quality during financial instability. This insight emphasizes the need for policymakers to integrate climate considerations into economic resilience strategies, particularly as climate change intensifies the frequency of such events [3][4].

Finally, the review of the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation underscores the EU's commitment to enhancing transparency in sustainable finance. Stricter disclosure requirements aim to align financial flows with sustainability goals, thereby mitigating greenwashing and fostering genuine investments.
