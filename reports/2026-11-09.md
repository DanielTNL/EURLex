**[1] [Unlocking supervisory effectiveness in credit risk: insights from the ECB’s supervisory findings and measures](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3308~a6e82e37aa.en.pdf)**

- This paper provides an assessment of supervisory effectiveness in credit risk using confidential data from ECB Banking Supervision.
- The analysis covers 8,106 supervisory measures issued and closed between 2016 and 2025.
- A central contribution is the construction of a measure level indicator of supervisory effectiveness based on large language model (LLM) analysis of supervisory narratives and closure justifications.
- The indicator captures whether supervisory measures resulted in full remediation of the underlying deficiencies.

**[3] [The EBA publishes consumer factsheet on white-labelled financial products and services](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-publishes-consumer-factsheet-white-labelled-financial-products-and-services)**

- The European Banking Authority (EBA) published today a factsheet to help consumers identify when a banking or payment product or service is white labelled, i.e.
- marketed under one brand but provided by another company.
- As consumers increasingly access financial products and services through online platforms, retailers and other non-financial brands, it may be sometimes unclear which company is responsible for providing the product or service.
- Understanding this distinction can help consumers make informed decisions, know who they are dealing with and reduce risks.

**[4] [From recognition to integration: the ECB’s progress on climate and nature-related risks and the role of its climate change centre](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/articles/2026/html/ecb.ebart202607_02~410d791050.en.html)**

- The ECB climate change centre (CCC) was established in 2021 to design and steer the ECB’s response to climate-related risks.
- During its first five years, the centre has evolved from a dedicated coordination hub into a facilitator of the integration of climate and nature-related risks into the ECB’s overall analytical and risk framework.
- It has thereby supported the institution’s progression from “recognition to integration” in addressing climate and nature-related risks.
- This article reviews the developments and achievements of the CCC and discusses how it continues to support the climate-related work of the ECB in response to a changing economic, policy and environmental landscape.
