### Briefing (~200 words)

Today’s through-line is straightforward: the EEA Joint Committee has continued its steady programme of “legal alignment by incorporation” in financial services, with one decision also extending that alignment into company law. Across five separate measures adopted on 5 December 2025, the EEA Agreement’s Annex IX (Financial services) is amended repeatedly, updating which EU rules apply in Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. In one case, Annex XXII (Company law) is amended as well, widening the compliance surface for corporate governance and related reporting obligations [4].

What changes, in practical terms, is not the existence of regulation but its content and the supervisory expectations attached to it. For firms, the implications are operational: internal governance, reporting lines, authorisation workflows and risk controls must be reviewed against the newly incorporated EU provisions. For regulators and supervisors, the shift is equally material—supervisory toolkits and compliance checklists need updating to reflect the amended annexed acts, affecting how licensing, conduct and prudential requirements are assessed in day-to-day supervision.

The market impact is therefore less about immediate trading effects and more about compliance timing and cross-border consistency. Because these decisions are published as EEA measures for application in 2026, stakeholders must track the incorporation and entry-into-force mechanics and any subsequent national administrative steps. The key risk is implementation lag: firms that plan only for EU timelines may find themselves out of sync with EEA-specific effective dates, while supervisors must avoid transitional ambiguity.

Overall, the day reinforces the EEA’s regulatory gravity: even without new EU legislation
