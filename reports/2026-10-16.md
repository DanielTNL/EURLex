**[1] [Stop believing in haircuts - at least the way you think about them](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3301~1585c96063.en.pdf)**

- We study how frictions impact haircut setting in repo markets.
- Using transaction-level supervisory data and exploiting the 2021 introduction of the binding leverage ratio requirement, we show that three frictions jointly shape haircuts in the bilateral dealer-to-client segment: dealer balance sheet constraints, collateral convenience yields, and client outside options.
- In collateral-driven repos, binding leverage constraints and broader client outside options both raise haircuts, while higher convenience yields push them down.
- In funding-driven trades, the leverage constraint has no effect, higher convenience yields raise haircuts through the rehypothecation channel, and broader client outside options lower them.

**[2] [When less is more: behavioral effects of CBDC holding limits](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3300~0277bae3af.en.pdf)**

- Holding limits are a central design feature of proposed retail central bank digital currencies (CBDC).
- While such limits mechanically prevent individuals from exceeding a maximum balance, they may also generate behavioral responses whose directions are a priori unclear.
- We conducted an incentivized online experiment with participants from four euro-area countries, exogenously varying the presence and level of a holding limit to causally separate direct mechanical effects from behavioral effects.
- We find that behavioral responses increase holdings of the means of payment subject to the holding limit, consistent with an attraction effect.
