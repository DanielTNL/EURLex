### CMU & Financial Markets

**[1] [Financial stability implications of AI adoption in the financial industry and the real economy](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/special/html/ecb.fsrart202611_01~dd6e15a84a.en.html)**

- The rapid adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping the financial industry as well as the broader economy, creating both opportunities and risks.
- Banks and asset managers, which represent key channels of financial intermediation in the euro area, are increasingly using AI for tasks such as credit scoring, fraud detection and portfolio management.
- While AI adoption can support innovation and enhance operational efficiency, it may also introduce new risks and amplify existing vulnerabilities, including a reliance on a small number of technology providers that creates concentration and third-party dependencies across the financial system.
- At the same time, financial institutions are also increasingly exposed to AI-driven developments in the real economy.

**[3] [Financial stability risks from large IPOs, equity issuance and passive investing](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/special/html/ecb.fsrart202611_03~46e116aeb8.en.html)**

- A new wave of equity issuance, concentrated in AI-related sectors, has brought public equity financing back to the forefront of capital markets.
- SpaceX’s record initial public offering (IPO) has led the way, with OpenAI and Anthropic planning to follow.
- This special feature assesses the financial stability implications of large IPOs and equity issuance in general, as well as how these interact with the trend towards passive investing.
- Primary equity financing supports financial stability by absorbing losses and limiting leverage at the firm level, but aggregate equity issuance is highly cyclical and strongly influenced by investor sentiment.
