**[4] [What analysts hear and why it matters for monetary policy](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3298~06485c09a7.en.pdf)**

- We introduce a novel measure of how European Central Bank (ECB) monetary policy is interpreted by analysts at major investment banks.
- We construct a “policy interpretation shock” based on the language used in analysts’ reports covering monetary policy decisions by the ECB’s Governing Council (GovC).
- The main identifying assumption is that changes in the way analysts report on policy communication by the GovC—that is, the change in the policy tone from the preview report to the recap report—reflect unexpected information revealed by the policy event.
- Using GPT-based sentiment extraction, we show that the tone in these reports closely tracks that of the GovC, often even anticipating shifts in tone.

**[16] [The heterogeneous bank lending channel of monetary policy](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3299~01776adb36.en.pdf)**

- How does heterogeneity in banks’ interest-rate risk exposure shape monetary policy transmission?
- We develop a quantitative macroeconomic model of heterogeneous banks to answer this question.
- We establish an irrelevance result: differences in interest-rate risk exposure between fixed- and variable-rate banking systems matter for transmission only when bank solvency concerns become relevant.
- Calibrating the model to the euro area, we show that idiosyncratic default risk pushes a substantial share of banks toward the solvency threshold, making heterogeneity quantitatively important.
