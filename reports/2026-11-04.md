**[34] [Debt maturity and government spending multipliers](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3306~028a82bc67.en.pdf)**

- Government spending effectiveness depends critically on how it is financed.
- Using state dependent SVAR models and local projections on post-war US data, we show that fiscal expansions financed with short-term debt generate significantly larger output multipliers than those financed with long-term debt.
- This difference mainly stems from private consumption responses: short-term financing crowds in consumption while long-term financing does not.
- Torationalize this finding, we construct an incomplete markets model in which households invest in short-term and long-term assets.

**[35] [Elastic in cash, inelastic in repo: hedge funds in the treasury and repo markets](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3305~877e68c2b7.en.pdf)**

- The functioning of sovereign bond markets is closely intertwined with repo markets, where investors finance long positions and source bonds for short sales.
- We show, theoretically and empirically, how conditions in the cash bond market fundamentally shape repo rates: When demand for cash bonds exceeds available supply, arbitrageurs, notably hedge funds, step in to bridge the gap by shorting bonds and borrowing them in the repo market.
- These arbitrage strategies drive a wedge between monetary policy rates and repo rates, generating “specialness”.
- To investigate the drivers of investors’ demand for repo contracts, we obtain regulatory data covering the entire universe of repos backed by German sovereign bonds.
