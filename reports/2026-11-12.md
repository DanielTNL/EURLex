**[1] [Narratives and risks: a quantitative toolkit to evaluate scenarios applied to the Middle East conflict](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202607_01~63d20abac5.en.html)**

- This box introduces a new quantitative methodology to evaluate alternative scenarios around the Eurosystem/ECB staff baseline projections, with a focus on their empirical plausibility.
- In a first step, the methodology constructs joint predictive distributions capturing temporal and cross-variable dynamics for euro area real GDP growth and HICP inflation.
- It then assesses the empirical plausibility of scenario outcomes by computing the likelihood of each scenario relative to that of the baseline against historical patterns.
- Applied to the Middle East scenarios in the September 2026 ECB staff macroeconomic projections, the framework shows a higher empirical plausibility for the milder scenario relative to the alternative scenarios.

**[2] [What share of public services employment is contributed by the private sector?](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202607_03~efdc9cea95.en.html)**

- This box examines the composition of “public services” employment in the euro area.
- This sector has contributed disproportionately to aggregate employment growth in recent years.
- Drawing on the ECB Consumer Expectations Survey and Eurostat’s EU Labour Force Survey, we show that only around half of the 6.9 million public services jobs created since 2008 can be attributed to the public sector.
- Within public services, health and social work is the dominant driver, yet this is also the least “publicly provided” sub-sector.

**[3] [Who holds stablecoins? New evidence on euro area consumer holdings](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202607_08~f4ddf0117c.en.html)**

- This box looks at whether stablecoins are on the rise in the euro area and what the implications are for financial stability, using data from the ECB’s Consumer Expectations Survey.
- It highlights the transactional nature of stablecoins and the risks posed by an increase in consumer holdings.
- While current exposures remain limited, the analysis underscores the importance of monitoring the use of stablecoins and the potential spillover effects on consumer confidence and financial markets.

**[4] [The real and financial effects of local corporate tax increases: evidence from linked firm–bank data](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3309~4e8af93b3a.en.pdf)**

- We study how local corporate tax increases affect firms’ financing and real activity.
- In Portugal, municipalities independently set a surtax on corporate income, generating plausibly exogenous variation across space and time.
- We link the universe of corporate balance sheets and profit-and-loss statements to loan-level data from the Bank of Portugal’s credit registry, allowing us to track firms’ liquidity, leverage, borrowing costs, and credit quality alongside revenues, employment, and productivity.
- We estimate local-projection difference-in-differences models that address staggered treatment timing and dynamic responses.
