### Briefing (~200 words)

Across today’s items, the through-line is the EU’s continued tightening of cross-border tax transparency through updated automatic exchange arrangements with a set of microstates and a major partner: Liechtenstein [1], San Marino [2], Monaco [3], Switzerland [4] and Andorra [5]. In each case, the European Parliament adopted a legislative resolution approving an *amending protocol* to an existing agreement. The change is therefore not a new reporting regime sprung into being overnight; it is an incremental upgrade to the legal framework that governs how financial institutions report account data and how tax authorities use it.

What has changed is the *standardisation and refresh* of the information-exchange rules to improve international tax compliance—explicitly aligned with the OECD’s latest approach in the Monaco file [3]. For markets, the implication is operational: banks and other reporting entities must be ready to adjust data collection, classification and transmission processes to match the amended protocol’s requirements. For taxpayers, the practical effect is a further reduction in the “information gap” for accounts linked to these jurisdictions, strengthening enforcement capacity and raising the cost of underreporting.

Regulatorially and institutionally, the day’s significance lies in the EU decision pipeline. These Parliament resolutions are “consultation” approvals for Council decisions; they provide the political and legal green light needed for the Union to conclude the protocols, with actual implementation contingent on Council adoption and entry into force. In short: the EU is not merely expanding transparency—it is continuously updating the machinery that makes transparency enforceable, with compliance burdens shifting quietly but steadily
