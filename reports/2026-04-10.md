**[29] [Monetary policy under multiple financing constraints](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3217~e43de9be7f.en.pdf)**

- The document advances a theory and empirical test of how monetary policy affects firms when they face more than one financing constraint, showing an asymmetric “credit channel”: easing is transmitted less to borrowing/investment, while tightening is transmitted more.  
- It argues the asymmetry comes from which constraint is binding: tightening makes the most responsive constraint bind, whereas easing leaves the least responsive one binding.  
- Using U.S. firm-level data and a quasi-natural experiment, it finds investment falls after contractionary shocks about twice as much as it rises after equally sized expansionary shocks, helping explain why tightenings often have stronger real-economy effects.  
- The implication is that policy effectiveness depends on the cross-firm distribution of constraints, and similar asymmetries may apply to other shocks; timing is tied to how quickly binding constraints switch after policy moves.
