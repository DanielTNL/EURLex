### Briefing (~200 words)

Today’s Official Journal trail is less about headline policy shifts than about *tightening the machinery* of EU regulation—turning broad legal concepts into enforceable, auditable rules across very different sectors.

In chemicals, the Commission amends REACH Annex XVII to tighten restrictions on **2,4-dinitrotoluene (2,4-DNT) in articles** [1]. The through-line is familiar: reduce exposure and risk, but do so by narrowing what firms can place on the market and under which conditions. For downstream manufacturers, importers and distributors, the change is operational as much as substantive—product formulations, documentation, and compliance testing must be re-checked against the updated restriction conditions. The market implication is a higher compliance cost and potential product redesign, with knock-on effects for supply-chain planning and legal risk.

In finance, the focus shifts from substances to *metrics*. A delegated regulation sets binding **technical standards for calculating market capitalisation and the market capitalisation ratio** under Directive (EU) 2025/50 [3]. By reducing discretion and improving comparability across Member States, it can influence how thresholds and eligibility decisions are applied in supervision and regulatory outcomes—forcing firms to recalibrate valuation models, data governance, and internal controls.

Finally, a corrigendum corrects regulatory technical standards for the **active account requirement** in the derivatives framework [5]. While narrower, it matters because supervisory reporting depends on precise wording: firms may need to adjust templates and representativeness evidence to avoid compliance errors.
