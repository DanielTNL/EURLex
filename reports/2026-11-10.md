**[1] [Industrial policy in the EU: new opportunities, old risks?](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/articles/2026/html/ecb.ebart202607_01~3ac9592e8b.en.html)**

- Motivated by the renewed use of industrial policy, this article examines the conditions under which sector-specific interventions can support productivity, competitiveness, and economic resilience.
- While industrial policy may be justified where market failures or dependencies are well-identified, selective intervention entails risks of distortion through rent-seeking and resource misallocation.
- These risks are particularly relevant in the EU, where uncoordinated industrial policy is implemented in a multi-country Single Market with heterogeneous institutions and bank-based financial structures.
- In this setting, industrial policy can be effective only with credible governance and complementary structural reforms.

**[2] [The EBA streamlines the 2027 EU-wide stress test with more than 55% reduction in requested data](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-streamlines-2027-eu-wide-stress-test-more-55-reduction-requested-data)**

- The European Banking Authority (EBA) today released the final methodology, draft templates, and template guidance for its 2027 EU-wide stress test, together with the key milestones for the exercise.
- Following extensive engagement with industry participants, the EBA has significantly streamlined the exercise, reducing the number of required data points by 55% through the simplification of reporting templates and closer alignment with supervisory reporting.
- These changes will reduce operational burden and improve efficiency while preserving the robustness, risk-sensitivity and comparability of stress test results.
- The 2027 exercise will also include, for the first time, a dedicated module assessing climate-related risks.
