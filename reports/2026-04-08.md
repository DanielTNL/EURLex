**[2] [Dynamic credit constraints: theory and evidence from credit lines](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3216~87bce3b9cf.en.pdf)**

- The document does not propose a policy change; it presents empirical evidence using Swedish credit-register data that firms—across small, medium and large sizes—have meaningful unused borrowing capacity through credit lines.  
- It explains the observed low credit-line utilization by estimating a structural firm-level model: utilization falls when uncertainty rises, but the dominant driver is financial uncertainty from liquidity shocks rather than real uncertainty from cash-flow shocks.  
- Firms are the affected “actors” in the analysis: most do not draw down available credit even when borrowing costs are low relative to their return on equity.  
- Why it matters: it shifts attention toward liquidity-risk management and financial stability measures, since reducing liquidity shocks is more likely to increase credit use than addressing real-economy uncertainty; timing is tied to the model’s shock-based interpretation rather than a specific implementation schedule.
