**[34] [Earnings manipulation and probability of default: insights from AnaCredit and supervisory](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op385.en.pdf)**

- The study tests whether earnings-manipulation indicators (Beneish M-Scores) show up in banks’ internal credit risk measures—probability of default (PD)—and whether any effect is immediate (point-in-time) or delayed (deferred).  
- Using AnaCredit credit-exposure data (2019–2022) plus Orbis financial statements, it links PD estimates to 4,649 publicly traded firms and finds a weak, negative overall correlation, implying manipulation signals are not fully incorporated into internal models.  
- When manipulation signals are present (8.9% of firms), PDs are higher and rise as M-Scores worsen, supporting a “deferred impact” rather than immediate reflection.  
- Results vary by country and industry, so implementation/oversight implications are context-dependent; the analysis period (2019–2022) frames when these dynamics are observed.
