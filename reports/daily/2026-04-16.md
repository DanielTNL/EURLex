## What is ELI? (and why EU innovation teams should care)

If you’ve ever tried to build a “single pane of glass” for EU law—say, to power compliance workflows, contract analytics, or regulatory monitoring—you’ll know the pain: identifiers are inconsistent, metadata is uneven, and machine-readability is often an afterthought. Enter **ELI: the European Legislation Identifier**—a standard designed to make EU legislation easier to find, reference and reuse across borders, for both humans *and* machines.

### 1) What happened and why it matters
The update you’re seeing isn’t a new funding pot or a defence procurement—it's a **standards and infrastructure story**. EUR-Lex is describing ELI as a framework that assigns every legal text a **universal resource identifier (URI)**, supported by structured metadata and a common data model. The practical outcome is straightforward: **legislation becomes easier to reference reliably in software, datasets and legal workflows**, reducing ambiguity and improving interoperability.

ELI is built on **four pillars** (which can be implemented together or progressively). The core idea is that legal documents should be:
- **Uniquely identifiable** via a consistent URI (so you can link to the same text everywhere).
- **Described with metadata** (type of legislation, adoption date, jurisdiction, etc.).
- **Modelled using a shared ontology/data model** (based on FRBR principles).
- **Published with machine-accessible distribution mechanisms** (including an ELI sitemap and Atom feed, plus a centralised graph concept).

EUR-Lex also flags that some features on the site are “experimental” and may affect stability—so the message is: **this is still being rolled out and refined**, but the direction is clear.

Why it matters for policy and finance audiences: **capital markets and innovation ecosystems live on information**. When legal texts are easier to identify and machine-process, it becomes easier to:
- automate regulatory mapping and change tracking,
- reduce manual legal research overhead,
- improve audit trails and reporting systems,
- and integrate compliance checks into product and investment workflows.

That’s not hype—it’s plumbing. But in regulated markets, plumbing is where time and cost savings hide.
