JOINT REPORT TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL
MACAO SPECIAL ADMINISTRATIVE REGION
ANNUAL REPORT FOR 2025
Summary
Since the handover of Macao to the People’s Republic of China (PRC) in 1999, the European Union (EU) and its Member States have closely followed political and economic developments in the Macao Special Administrative Region (SAR) under the ‘one country, two systems’ principle. The EU supports this principle and its implementation, in accordance with the Basic Law, which is intended to ensure that the way of life, rule of law and protection of fundamental rights and freedoms in Macao remain unchanged until 2049. In line with the commitment given to the European Parliament in 1999, the Commission and the High Representative/Vice-President issue an annual report on developments in Macao. Over the years, this has included a particular focus on the implementation of the ‘one country, two systems’ principle on Macao’s autonomy as stated in the Macao Basic Law. This is the 26th report, covering 2025. Politically, the year saw a continued erosion of autonomy and fundamental freedoms and rights that are meant to be protected until at least 2049. The year was marked by: (i) the eighth Legislative Assembly election, and (ii) the first arrest under the Law on Safeguarding of National Security. The EU Spokesperson issued a statement covering the following issue:
· 2 August 2025 – Statement by the Spokesperson on the arrest of Au Kam San 1
The GDP of Macao increased by 4.7% year on year in 2025. This was the third consecutive year of growth, but the economy has yet to fully recover to pre-COVID-19 pandemic levels. The economic growth was supported by increases in the numbers of tourists travelling to Macao and related gaming revenues. Macao reported 40.1 million visitors in 2025. This is the first time since the end of the COVID-19 pandemic that the number of tourists surpassed pre-pandemic levels. However, despite the increase in tourists, gaming revenues did not recover to pre-pandemic level. In 2025, the EU was Macao’s second-largest trading partner in goods, accounting for 26.0% of Macao’s total trade and with a trade surplus in favour of the EU.

JOINT REPORT TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL
HONG KONG SPECIAL ADMINISTRATIVE REGION:
ANNUAL REPORT FOR 2025
Summary
Since Hong Kong’s handover to the People’s Republic of China in 1997, the European Union (EU) and its Member States have closely followed political and economic developments in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (SAR). In line with the commitment given to the European Parliament in 1997, the Commission and the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy/Vice-President issue an annual report on developments in Hong Kong. Over the years, this has included a particular focus on the implementation of the ‘one country, two systems’ principle and on Hong Kong’s autonomy as stated in Hong Kong’s own Basic Law. This is the 28th report, covering 2025. In 2025 Hong Kong remained the world’s third-largest financial centre (and largest in Asia) and an important trading and logistics hub. About 1 770 EU companies were active in Hong Kong, making the EU the largest non-Chinese foreign business presence in the SAR. About half of those companies were running regional operations from Hong Kong. Nationals of EU Member States continued to make significant contributions to the SAR’s economy and society, including in science, education and culture. Hong Kong’s common-law system, and a low and simple tax system are features that help maintain its attractiveness for international companies. Politically, the year was marked by: (i) additional subsidiary national security legislation, (ii) the second ‘patriots-only’ Legislative Council election featuring a political vetting mechanism, (iii) rejection by the Legislative Council of a proposed bill recognising same-sex partnerships and (iv) an ongoing focus on national security in the fifth year after the imposition of the National Security Law, including high profile court cases. The erosion of Hong Kong’s high degree of autonomy and of the fundamental freedoms and rights of the people of Hong Kong continued, despite China’s legal obligation to maintain them until at least 2047. In 2025, trials continued against pro-democracy activists, advocates and politicians under the 2020 National Security Law (NSL) and other legislation. In December, the court convicted media entrepreneur and pro-democracy advocate Jimmy Lai on national security and sedition charges.
