1. CONTEXT OF THE PROPOSAL
• Reasons for and objectives of the proposal
Advanced materials are essential to the technologies that will shape Europe’s future economic strength, industrial leadership and strategic autonomy. They are key to closing the innovation gap, attracting investments and reshaping resilient global supply chains. The European Commission’s Competitiveness Compass 1 and its EU Startup and Scaleup Strategy 2 identify advanced materials as a strategic technological area. Advanced materials had already been recognised as one of the ten critical technology areas for the Union’s economic security and were addressed in a related risk assessment carried out with Member States 3 . Advanced materials are materials that are rationally designed to deliver new or enhanced properties, targeted or enhanced structural features, or both, with the objective of achieving specific or improved functional performance. They include new emerging manufactured materials, materials manufactured from conventional materials, and materials produced through innovative manufacturing processes that enable the creation of targeted structures from starting materials 4 . Advanced materials improve the performance of products and technologies and are key enablers for the green, clean, digital transitions and the EU defence readiness, thus also allowing the Union to achieve its policy targets. They underpin innovation in strategic sectors, including energy, mobility, aerospace, defence, construction, electronics or healthcare. By strengthening industrial competitiveness, supporting sustainable prosperity and reducing strategic dependencies, they also contribute to the EU’s strategic autonomy. Examples of advanced material applications are thermochromic nanoparticles for smart glass windows that dynamically adjust their tint to ensure buildings’ energy-efficiency, novel magnetic alloys (including high-entropy alloys and composite ferrites) for permanent magnets with applications in energy and automotive that can reduce dependencies on critical raw materials, high-performance membranes for the removal of PFAS (that are per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances or so-called ‘forever chemicals’), prevention, detection and repair of water leakages and water purification, or light and high-strength composites, including biobased, with graphene for applications in aerospace, automotive and construction. Innovation in these sectors can also benefit other sectors through the cross-cutting application of advanced materials, as for example in agriculture, water and security. The EU’s ability to achieve its climate, water resilience, digital, defence readiness and competitiveness objectives increasingly depends on reinforcing its leadership in advanced materials. Global competition in the development, manufacturing and securing of advanced materials is intensifying rapidly.

ANNUAL REVIEW BY THE COMMISSION
of Member States' Annual Activity Reports on Export Credits in the sense of Regulation (EU) No 1233/2011
1. Introduction:
This annual review for the year 2025 on officially supported export credit activities is produced pursuant to Regulation (EU) No 1233/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 November 2011 on the application of certain guidelines in the field of officially supported export credits. It covers officially supported export credit activities carried out by the Export Credit Agencies (ECAs) of the Member States and is based on the information provided by Member States in the Annual Activity Reports they make available to the Commission, also pursuant to the Regulation. The reports follow a standardised template (also referred to as a “checklist template”) agreed with the Member States which has been reviewed and enriched over time. The current template is used since 2023 and includes additional details, in particular a sector-by-sector climate-oriented review of officially supported export credits activities, as well as more harmonized financial reporting. 2. Annual Activity Reports received for the 2025 calendar year:
Twenty-one Member States were able to provide in 2025 officially supported export credits within the scope of application of the Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits (“the Arrangement”): Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Greece, Germany, Hungary, Italy, Luxemburg, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovenia, Slovakia, Spain and Sweden. All submitted an Annual Activity Report for the year 2025. The remaining Member States - Bulgaria, Cyprus, Ireland, Latvia, Lithuania and Malta – confirmed that they do not have an agency able to provide officially supported export credits. 3. Analysis of the Annual Activity Reports:
(a) General information:
In 2025, all Member States with at least one ECA except for Greece (whose ECA does not provide any financial support in the scope of the Arrangement) provided pure cover, meaning export credits in the form of guarantees and/or insurance products. Of these, thirteen Member States (Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Hungary, Italy, Poland, Slovakia, Spain and Sweden) were also providers of official financing support, meaning direct financing, re-financing of commercial loans for export transactions or interest rate support.
