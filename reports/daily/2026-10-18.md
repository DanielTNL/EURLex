REPORT FROM THE COMMISSION TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL
Sixth Annual Report on the screening of foreign direct investments into the Union
INTRODUCTION
This report is the European Commission’s sixth annual report on the application of the EU Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Screening Regulation (the ‘FDI Screening Regulation’ or the ‘Regulation’). The report covers 2025 and provides transparency on the operation of FDI screening in the EU and developments in national screening mechanisms. It contributes to the accountability of the EU in an area where, given the security interests at stake, transparency on individual transactions is neither possible nor appropriate. It is based on reports by the 27 Member States and official statistics and consists of four chapters:
§ Chapter 1 on trends and figures for FDI into the EU;
§ Chapter 2 on legislative developments in Member States;
§ Chapter 3 on FDI screening activities carried out by Member States;
§ Chapter 4 on the EU cooperation mechanism on FDI screening. This annual report is an important transparency tool highlighting trends, development and action regarding FDI in order to ensure security and public order in the EU. CHAPTER 1 – FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT INTO THE EUROPEAN UNION
1. Overall evolution 1
The stock of foreign direct investment (FDI) 2 increased by 8.9% between 2024 and 2025. Foreign mergers and acquisitions (M&A) and greenfield projects experienced annual cumulative increases of 12% (from 20,695 to 23,078) and 6.9% (from 27,069 to 28,930) respectively, as shown by the bars in Figure 1 . This increase is consistent with trends observed since 2016, with the stock of FDI into the EU27 continuously increasing over this 10-year period by an average of around 5,200 FDIs per year. Looking at this cumulated trend by FDI type, the EU27 received an average of 2,308 foreign M&A deals, and 2,893 greenfield investments each year between 2016 and 2025. These positive cumulative trends confirm the openness of the Union to foreign investments, highlighting the attractiveness of the EU Single Market to international investors.

REPORT FROM THE COMMISSION TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL
on the application of Regulation (EU) 2019/1240 of 20 June 2019 on the creation of a European network of immigration liaison officers
1. Introduction
1.1 Policy context
The European network of immigration liaison officers (the ‘ILO Network’) is the community of liaison officers posted to third countries by Member States, Schengen associated countries, EU home affairs agencies ( 1 ) and the European Commission (‘ILO Network Members’) to work full-time or part-time on immigration tasks. The overall aim of the ILO Network is to ensure optimal cooperation and coordination between immigration liaison officers (‘ILOs’) in support of EU policy in the field of migration. The ILO Network, which was set up in 2004 by Council Regulation (EC) No 377/2004 ( 2 ), was reinforced by Regulation (EU) 2019/1240 ( 3 ) on the creation of a European network of immigration liaison officers (‘the ILO Network Regulation’). The ILO Network Regulation is aimed at modernising and strengthening the ILO Network to enable it to play a more effective role in EU migration management, including by fostering a Team Europe approach within the Network. It broadened the definition of the concept of ‘immigration liaison officer’, and introduced other innovative aspects, such as a new ILO Network Steering Board ( 4 ) and a stronger role for liaison officers deployed by the Commission and EU home affairs agencies. It strengthened the role played by ILOs in gathering and exchanging information, including by establishing a secure web-based information exchange platform, and broadened the ILO Network’s scope to embrace the full spectrum of migration management tasks. The ILO Network Regulation is an essential part of the EU’s comprehensive approach to migration management under the Pact on Migration and Asylum (‘the Pact’) that entered into application on 12 June 2026 ( 5 ). The ILO Network plays a key role in embedding migration in international partnerships, which is a cornerstone of the Pact.
